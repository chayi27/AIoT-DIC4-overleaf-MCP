\begin{abstract}
Dietary self-monitoring significantly improves weight management and metabolic health outcomes; however, conventional calorie-tracking applications impose severe manual logging friction, resulting in an 85\% user abandonment rate within 30 days and self-reported intake errors of 18\%--54\%. While general-purpose large language models (LLMs) offer natural conversational interfaces, they lack auditable nutritional database anchoring and exhibit a 35.8\% mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) alongside numerical hallucinations. To address this gap, we present \textit{DietSense}, a conversational Domain AI Agent tailored for Taiwanese daily dietary tracking. DietSense enforces a strict architectural decoupling principle: \textit{LLM for Understanding and Orchestration; Domain Engine for Computation}. The proposed eight-module pipeline integrates speech-to-text transcription, parallel three-tier nutritional retrieval (combining USDA FoodData Central, the Taiwan Food and Drug Administration database, and a localized cuisine vector store), a single-turn optimal clarification loop triggered below a 0.80 confidence threshold, uncertainty propagation yielding honest confidence intervals, and non-bypassable clinical safety guardrails (blocking daily intake recommendations below 1,200~kcal for females and 1,500~kcal for males). We further specify a 500-dish annotated Taiwanese cuisine benchmark and a 7-day, 20-participant evaluation protocol targeting $>$85\% accuracy within $\pm$20\% error, a 60\% reduction in logging time (from 85~s to under 35~s), and a System Usability Scale (SUS) score above 70.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
According to the World Health Organization (WHO), global obesity has surpassed one billion people, and diet-related chronic diseases---including cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and hypertension---account for 74\% of global mortality \cite{who_obesity}. In Taiwan, the prevalence of overweight and obesity among adults aged 18 and older has reached 47.9\%, generating over NT\$50~billion in annual direct and indirect healthcare expenditures \cite{hpa_taiwan}. Clinical and behavioral studies demonstrate that consistent dietary self-monitoring doubles the success rate of weight management and metabolic health maintenance \cite{burke_self_monitoring}. Nevertheless, the cognitive burden of searching food items, weighing portions, and manually entering ingredients causes up to 85\% of users to abandon conventional tracking tools within 30 days \cite{laing_abandonment}.

Existing commercial trackers such as MyFitnessPal rely heavily on manual keyword lookup and user-generated crowdsourced databases, which suffer from high interaction friction, noisy entries, and poor coverage of localized Taiwanese street foods, bento meals, and composite soups. Under such friction, self-reported dietary intake errors commonly range from 18\% to 54\% \cite{schoeller_selfreport}. Conversely, general-purpose large language models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT and Claude excel at parsing colloquial meal descriptions (e.g., ``a bowl of braised beef noodle soup with an egg, drinking half the broth''), yet they lack real-time, auditable nutritional database grounding. When prompted directly for calorie estimation, general LLMs exhibit a mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 35.8\% and frequently produce \textit{numerical hallucinations}---fabricating seemingly precise single-point figures without scientific provenance \cite{llm_nutrition_mape}.

To bridge this gap between conversational usability and clinical-grade nutritional accuracy, we propose \textbf{DietSense}, a conversational Domain AI Agent built on the core architectural principle: \textit{LLM for Understanding and Orchestration; Domain Engine for Computation}. Rather than treating the user as a passive database clerk or allowing an LLM to guess numbers, DietSense delegates semantic parsing, intent routing, and multi-turn clarification to the LLM, while routing all energy density, basal metabolic rate (BMR), total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), and uncertainty interval calculations to deterministic domain algorithms.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Agentic Architecture}: An eight-module pipeline that strictly isolates LLM semantic orchestration from deterministic nutritional computation and clinical safety checks.
    \item \textbf{Three-Tier Localized Data Anchoring}: A hierarchical retrieval strategy integrating Taiwan TFDA and USDA FoodData Central ground-truth tables, a 500-dish nutritionist-verified Taiwanese cuisine RAG vector store, and dynamic composite recipe decomposition.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty-Aware Clarification Loop}: A sensitivity-prioritized, single-turn clarification mechanism (triggered at confidence $< 0.80$) paired with error-propagated confidence intervals instead of false-precision point estimates.
    \item \textbf{Hard Clinical Safety Guardrails}: Non-bypassable physiological intake floors ($1,200\text{~kcal/day}$ for females, $1,500\text{~kcal/day}$ for males), eating-disorder interception, and guilt-free positive cross-meal reframing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Conventional and Crowdsourced Calorie Trackers}
Mobile applications such as MyFitnessPal, Lose It!, and MyNetDiary provide extensive food catalogs via user-generated content (UGC). However, UGC databases frequently contain duplicate entries, unit mismatches, and severe inaccuracies (e.g., mislabeling a full noodle dish as 50~kcal), while lacking standardized Taiwanese street-food compositions. Moreover, manual entry averages approximately 85 seconds per meal, driving high 30-day attrition \cite{laing_abandonment}.

\subsection{Vision-Based Dietary Assessment}
Computer vision approaches estimate food categories and portion volumes from meal photographs \cite{vision_food}. While convenient, single-view images cannot reliably infer hidden caloric contributors prevalent in East Asian cuisine, such as broth oil content, cooking oil absorption in stir-fried or deep-fried items, or braised sauce sugar levels.

\subsection{LLMs and Domain-Specific AI Agents in Healthcare}
General-purpose LLMs demonstrate strong zero-shot natural language understanding but suffer from uncalibrated numerical estimation \cite{llm_nutrition_mape}. Recent agentic frameworks employ Function Calling and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{lewis_rag} to ground LLM outputs in external tools. DietSense advances this paradigm by combining multi-source nutritional conflict resolution, cooking yield and oil absorption modeling, uncertainty propagation, and deterministic clinical safety guardrails within a unified conversational workflow.

\section{System Architecture and Methodology}
\subsection{Eight-Module Architecture}
DietSense is structured into eight decoupled functional modules, summarized in Table~\ref{tab:modules}. Developed under a Spec-Driven Development (SDD) workflow, module boundaries and safety invariants are formally specified prior to implementation to prevent specification drift.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{DietSense Eight-Module Functional Architecture}
\label{tab:modules}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clp{4.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Module Name} & \textbf{Core Responsibility} \\ \midrule
M1 & User Scenario & Captures unstructured voice/text meal descriptions in daily colloquial language. \\
M2 & Input Processing & Performs Whisper ASR transcription, Traditional/Simplified Chinese normalization, and intent classification. \\
M3 & Planner \& Reasoning & LLM orchestrator managing multi-turn context, Function Calling, and Clarification Loop ($\text{conf} < 0.80$). \\
M4 & Data Retrieval & Parallel query pipeline across USDA API, Taiwan TFDA database, and Taiwanese cuisine RAG store. \\
M5 & Analytics Engine & Computes energy density, Mifflin-St Jeor / Katch-McArdle BMR/TDEE, yield/oil adjustments, and CI. \\
M6 & Domain Knowledge & Encodes Taiwan Daily Dietary Guidelines, 6-group food exchanges, and clinical safety rules. \\
M7 & Decision \& Advice & Evaluates daily TDEE gaps and generates guilt-free, positive next-meal substitution guidance. \\
M8 & Output \& Interaction & Renders structured Markdown cards, macronutrient charts, data provenance IDs, and disclaimers. \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Core Mathematical Formulations}
All nutritional quantities are computed deterministically by the Analytics Engine (M5):

\subsubsection{Food Energy Density}
Given an estimated cooked portion weight $W_{\text{portion}}$ (in grams) and database energy density $\text{kcal}_{100\text{g}}$:
\begin{equation}
E = \frac{W_{\text{portion}} \times \text{kcal}_{100\text{g}}}{100}
\label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Basal Metabolic Rate and TDEE}
For general users, DietSense adopts the clinically validated Mifflin-St Jeor equations \cite{mifflin_1990}:
\begin{align}
\text{BMR}_{\text{male}} &= 10w + 6.25h - 5a + 5 \label{eq:bmr_m} \\
\text{BMR}_{\text{female}} &= 10w + 6.25h - 5a - 161 \label{eq:bmr_f}
\end{align}
where $w$ is weight (kg), $h$ is height (cm), and $a$ is age (years). Total Daily Energy Expenditure is then derived as:
\begin{equation}
\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} \times \alpha_{\text{act}}, \quad \alpha_{\text{act}} \in [1.2, 1.9]
\label{eq:tdee}
\end{equation}
When body-fat percentage $f_{\text{body}}$ is available, M5 switches to the Katch-McArdle equation \cite{katch_mcardle} to avoid underestimating BMR in muscular individuals:
\begin{equation}
\text{BMR}_{\text{KM}} = 370 + 21.6 \times (1 - f_{\text{body}}) \times w
\label{eq:bmr_km}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, a 14-day exponential moving average tracker detects adaptive thermogenesis when two consecutive weeks of theoretical 1~kg deficit yield no weight change (excluding edema), adjusting TDEE downward by 5\%--8\%.

\subsubsection{Cooking Yield and Oil Absorption}
Raw ingredient equivalence accounts for cooking yield factors ($\text{YF}$), such as 0.85 for boiled leafy greens, 0.75 for pan-seared meats, and 2.3 for steamed rice:
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{raw}} = \frac{W_{\text{cooked}}}{\text{YF}}
\label{eq:yield}
\end{equation}
Cooking oil absorption is modeled as a step function over detected cooking verbs: steaming/boiling (+0\%), stir-frying (+5\%--8\% fat weight), battered deep-frying (+12\%--18\% fat weight), and high-absorption matrices such as fried dough sticks or battered eggplant (up to +25\% fat weight), adding $9\text{~kcal/g}$ deterministically.

\subsubsection{Uncertainty Propagation}
Rather than presenting false-precision single numbers, DietSense propagates portion estimation variance $\sigma_{\text{portion}}$ and database composition variance $\sigma_{\text{db}}$:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\text{total}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\text{portion}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{db}}^2}
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
mapping to $\sigma_{\text{total}} \approx 0.05$ for exact gram inputs, $0.15$ for container-based descriptions (e.g., ``one bowl''), and $0.35$ for abstract descriptions (e.g., ``some snacks''). The reported calorie interval is:
\begin{equation}
[\text{kcal}_{\text{low}}, \text{kcal}_{\text{high}}] = \left[E(1 - \sigma_{\text{total}}),\; E(1 + \sigma_{\text{total}})\right]
\label{eq:ci}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Core Orchestration Algorithm}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:dietsense} details the end-to-end execution flow of the DietSense agent across intent classification, multi-source retrieval, single-turn clarification, deterministic calculation, and clinical safety interception.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{DietSense Daily Calorie Intake Domain AI Agent}
\label{alg:dietsense}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require User Query $Q$, User Profile $P$, Daily Log $D$
\Ensure Formatted Response $R$, Updated Daily Log $D'$
\State $Q_{\text{norm}} \gets \textsc{NormalizeInput}(Q)$
\State $\text{intent} \gets \textsc{ClassifyIntent}(Q_{\text{norm}})$ \Comment{\texttt{log\_food}, \texttt{query\_nutrition}, \texttt{analyze}, \texttt{recommend}}
\If{$\text{intent} = \text{``log\_food''}$}
    \State $\mathcal{E} \gets \textsc{ExtractFoodEntities}(Q_{\text{norm}})$
    \For{each $\text{food} \in \mathcal{E}$}
        \State $\mathcal{C} \gets \textsc{QueryUSDA}(\text{food}) \cup \textsc{QueryTFDA}(\text{food}) \cup \textsc{QueryRAG}(\text{food})$
        \State $(\text{best}, \text{conf}) \gets \textsc{RankCandidates}(\mathcal{C}, \text{food.context})$
        \If{$\text{conf} < 0.80$} \Comment{Single-turn optimal clarification}
            \State $q_{\text{clar}} \gets \textsc{GenerateClarification}(\text{food}, \mathcal{C})$
            \State $a_{\text{user}} \gets \textsc{AskUser}(q_{\text{clar}})$
            \State $\text{food} \gets \textsc{UpdateEntity}(\text{food}, a_{\text{user}})$
            \State $(\text{best}, \text{conf}) \gets \textsc{RankCandidates}(\textsc{QueryAll}(\text{food}), \text{food.context})$
        \EndIf
        \State $W_g \gets \textsc{EstimatePortionGrams}(\text{food.portion}, \text{best})$
        \State $E_{\text{pt}} \gets W_g \times \text{best.kcal}_{100\text{g}} / 100$
        \State $(E_{\text{low}}, E_{\text{high}}) \gets \textsc{ComputeCI}(E_{\text{pt}}, \text{conf}, \text{food.portion})$
        \State $D.\textsc{AddEntry}(\text{best}, W_g, (E_{\text{low}}, E_{\text{pt}}, E_{\text{high}}))$
    \EndFor
\ElsIf{$\text{intent} \in \{\text{``analyze''}, \text{``recommend''}\}$}
    \State $E_{\text{daily}} \gets \sum_{e \in D.\text{entries}} e.E_{\text{pt}}$
    \State $\text{BMR} \gets \textsc{CalcBMR}(P.\text{weight}, P.\text{height}, P.\text{age}, P.\text{gender})$
    \State $\text{TDEE} \gets \text{BMR} \times P.\text{activity\_factor}$
    \If{$\textsc{SafetyCheck}(D, P) = \text{``DANGER''}$} \Comment{$<1200/1500$~kcal or ED risk}
        \State $\text{alert} \gets \textsc{GenerateSafetyAlert}(D, P)$
        \State \Return $\textsc{FormatResponse}(\text{alert}),\; D$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\State $R \gets \textsc{FormatResponse}(\text{intent}, D, \textsc{GenerateCharts}(D))$
\State \Return $R,\; D$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Architectural Trade-Offs and Key Edge-Case Mechanisms}
To balance real-world usability with clinical rigor, DietSense resolves three fundamental architectural trade-offs (Table~\ref{tab:tradeoffs}):

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Summary of Core Architectural Trade-Offs in DietSense}
\label{tab:tradeoffs}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.2cm}p{2.7cm}p{3.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Trade-Off} & \textbf{Underlying Tension} & \textbf{DietSense Strategy} \\ \midrule
Accuracy vs.\ UX & Over-questioning causes 85\% attrition; zero questioning yields $>50\%$ error. & Confidence threshold ($0.80$), single-turn button clarification, sensible defaults, and CI display. \\
Quantity vs.\ Quality & UGC databases are noisy; official tables lack Taiwanese composite dishes. & 3-tier anchoring (TFDA/USDA $\rightarrow$ 500-dish RAG $\rightarrow$ dynamic recipe decomposition). \\
General vs.\ Personalized & Rigid public guidelines feel irrelevant; extreme personalization risks eating disorders. & Mifflin-St Jeor TDEE adaptation bounded by hard clinical floors ($<1,200/1,500$~kcal). \\ \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity-Prioritized Single-Turn Clarification}: When parsing ambiguous inputs (e.g., beef noodle soup with egg), M3 ranks latent variables by caloric variance ($\Delta \text{kcal}_{\text{broth}} \approx 180\text{~kcal}$, $\Delta \text{kcal}_{\text{egg}} \approx 90\text{~kcal}$, $\Delta \text{kcal}_{\text{noodle}} \approx 40\text{~kcal}$) and issues at most one button-based clarification turn, falling back gracefully to local defaults if skipped.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Conflict Resolution}: Conflicting database values are resolved via geographical proximity weighting:
    \begin{equation}
    W_{\text{TFDA}} (0.5) > W_{\text{Taiwan\_RAG}} (0.4) > W_{\text{USDA}} (0.1)
    \end{equation}
    supported by a semantic unit harmonizer mapping Taiwanese colloquial measures (e.g., a standard 250--300~ml snack bowl or one \textit{liang} $= 37.5\text{~g}$) to grams.
    \item \textbf{Three-Stage Graceful Degradation}: If cloud RAG or external APIs time out (Level~2), the system falls back to a local SQLite cache of 1,000 high-frequency Taiwanese foods using fuzzy regex matching; if LLM quotas are exhausted (Level~3), raw text logs are queued locally for asynchronous synchronization.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Safety Net and Guilt-Free Reframing}: Hard guardrails block recommendations below $1,200\text{~kcal/day}$ (female) or $1,500\text{~kcal/day}$ (male), intercept 3-day deficits exceeding $1,000\text{~kcal/day}$, and trigger medical referral messages upon detecting purging or fasting keywords. For routine caloric surpluses, M7 avoids punitive warnings (mitigating the ``what-the-hell effect'') and suggests balanced, sodium-conscious dinner alternatives, accompanied by database provenance tags and regulatory disclaimers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup and Evaluation Plan}
In strict accordance with academic integrity, no synthetic or preliminary experimental results are fabricated in this proposal. Instead, we define the complete benchmark, baselines, human evaluation protocol, and target acceptance criteria for empirical validation.

\subsection{Benchmark Dataset and Baselines}
We construct a \textbf{500-item Taiwanese Local Dish Benchmark Dataset} covering night-market snacks (e.g., Taiwanese fried chicken, oyster omelet, sweet potato balls), everyday bento meals (e.g., fried pork chop bento, braised pork rice), and composite noodle soups (e.g., braised beef noodle soup, danzai noodles). Each entry is annotated by qualified nutritionists with standard gram weights, raw-to-cooked yield factors, and Taiwan TFDA reference values.

DietSense will be evaluated against two baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline 1 (Conventional Tracker)}: MyFitnessPal with manual keyword search and portion selection.
    \item \textbf{Baseline 2 (General LLM)}: Zero-shot GPT-4o calorie estimation without external database anchoring (previously reported at 35.8\% MAPE \cite{llm_nutrition_mape}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Human Study Protocol and Evaluation Metrics}
We plan to recruit \textbf{20 participants} (aged 20--50 years, spanning office workers, weight-management users, and regular exercisers) for a \textbf{7-day} real-world dietary logging study. Evaluation metrics include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Caloric Estimation Accuracy}: Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) and attainment rate within a $\pm 20\%$ error tolerance band.
    \item \textbf{Logging Efficiency}: Mean logging duration per meal in seconds.
    \item \textbf{System Usability}: Standardized 100-point System Usability Scale (SUS) \cite{brooke_sus}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Expected Performance Targets}
Upon completion of the 7-day trial, DietSense is designed to achieve: (1) $>85\%$ attainment within the $\pm 20\%$ calorie tolerance band (substantially outperforming the 64.2\% baseline of general LLMs); (2) a $60\%$ reduction in per-meal logging time, from an average of 85~s in conventional tools to under 35~s; and (3) a SUS usability score $>70$.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented \textit{DietSense}, a conversational Domain AI Agent designed to overcome the high abandonment rates of manual calorie trackers and the numerical hallucinations of general-purpose LLMs in Taiwanese dietary management. By decoupling LLM semantic orchestration from deterministic nutritional computation, anchoring queries in a three-tier localized database pipeline, quantifying estimation uncertainty via confidence intervals, and enforcing non-bypassable clinical safety guardrails, DietSense establishes a transparent and trustworthy framework for everyday health informatics. Planned open-source deliverables include the complete Python agent codebase, the 500-dish annotated Taiwanese cuisine dataset, and the formal system architecture blueprint (\texttt{DESIGN.md}), paving the way for reproducible empirical validation in the 20-participant field study.
